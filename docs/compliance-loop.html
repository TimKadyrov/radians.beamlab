Inside the Compliance Loop

The loop answers two questions about the operated system: does it meet the Article 22 epfd(down) limits, latitude by latitude — and if not, what is the smallest GSO-exclusion discipline that does? Whatever it finds is written back into the operation profile, so the declaration and the simulated behaviour stay one thing.

The loop, end to end

The sweep

A victim earth station is planted at each latitude of a grid (default 0–70° in 10° steps) at your chosen ES longitude, with the wanted GSO satellite at a fixed longitude offset and an S.1428 dish. Each victim gets the same truth run the Simulation runner performs — the live scheduled beam composition, or, when the profile declares the mask footprint source, the examination's own read of the declared PFD mask (Recommendation ITU-R S.1503-4 §D5.1.4.1). Each latitude becomes one row: the maximum epfd seen, the verdict, and the worst dB margin. Export table… writes the rows as CSV. While a sweep runs, the status line and a progress bar report each latitude as it starts and finishes (with its verdict and margin) and the fraction of time steps done within it; the advisors add one line per walk step, so a long run on a large constellation is never silent.

Tech · verdict and margin

The verdict follows the rule of the dataset design brief, compliance against the Article 22 limit curve. Two tests, both required. At every tabulated limit point the simulated level exceeded for the allowed percentage of time must not pass the limit level (S.1503-4 §D7.1.3, over the §D7.1.2 CDF bins). And between the lowest and highest tabulated epfd the distribution must nowhere cross the limit curve, the log-linear interpolation of percentage between adjacent points, read with a tolerance of half a bin (0.05 dB) towards lower epfd. The gaps between tabulated points are wide — 5.4 to 34 dB in Table 22-1C — and a distribution can clear both ends of a gap and bulge above the curve inside it; the Bureau’s reference implementation fails such a case, so the point-wise test alone is the lenient reading. Two margins are reported. The point margin is the limit epfd minus the measured epfd at each point’s percentage, minimum over the points; the curve margin is the dB shift of the whole distribution that just clears the curve. The verdict’s margin is the smaller of the two; positive means room to spare, and the worst row is the binding latitude. Every record names the rule and the tolerance it verdicts under.

The limits

The sweep verdicts against the limit points in the text box — one epfd_db percent line per point. Type them from the applicable Article 22 table, or load them: point at the BR limits database and Load reads the rows applicable to the profile's downlink carrier and the shells' lowest operating height; pick a row and Use fills the text. Each row names its reference bandwidth and its own reference dish diameter — set the victim dish to the row's diameter, since that is the antenna the limit was written for. Latitude-dependent short-term rows cannot fit the flat text and are shown for hand transcription of your latitude band.

Which failure is it?

A failed verdict means different things depending on which projection failed, and the remedy differs accordingly. The truth run T and the examination runs E1/E2 sit in a guaranteed order — a faithful declaration envelopes the truth, so E1 ≥ T at every percentile — which leaves exactly three cases. The basis for the rule: the examination's level is never its own number — it is the truth plus the projection margin the declaration adds — so a crossed limit line is crossed either by the truth itself or inside that added margin, and the fix belongs to the component that crossed it:

  • T fails. The operation itself violates the limits, and no declaration work is legitimate — tuning the filing would only paper over a real problem. Fix the system first: per-beam power density is the exact lever (epfd moves dB-for-dB, and the sweep already prints how much per-beam power must be shed), then the operating rules this window walks (exclusion angle, caps), and only then the constellation itself.
  • T passes, E1 fails. The operation is fine; the filing would be found non-compliant. This is the projection-margin regime, and the optimisation moves to the declaration side — in this order: refine the examination inputs (mask b/c and latitude grids, a time step fine enough to resolve the quoted percentiles — the measured decomposition put several dB in these), tighten the declarations to the simulated truth (the deriver's envelopes rather than hand-written generosity), and promote enforced rules so the reachable envelope itself shrinks. The goal is fidelity — declarations at the granularity that reflects the real operation. Passing is the consequence when the system deserves it, never a target the declarations are fitted to.
  • Both pass. File; the remaining E1 − T margin is conservatism the filing carries as robustness.

There is no fourth case: E1 ≥ T means the examination can never green-light a system whose truth is red. If a run ever shows E1 below T at some percentile, that is not good news — it is a mis-declaration (the envelope does not envelope), and the direction check exists to catch exactly that.

The advisor

Suggest exclusion walks the global exclusion angle from the profile's current value upward in your step, re-running the full sweep at each value, and stops at the first fully compliant angle. The walk is deliberately linear, not a bisection, because the walk itself is an output: every intermediate sweep is a point on the margin-versus-alpha trajectory, and the failure report uses it — the worst margin at the end, the latitude holding it, and whether it was improving, worsening or not moving with alpha. From the trend it says what to do next: raise the cap and keep walking, accept that a larger exclusion worsens this geometry, or recognise that the exclusion is not the binding lever and the system itself must change.

Apply to profile then writes the found angle into the profile file. That is the loop's invariant: the advisor's exclusion becomes scheduler-enforced operation, never an advisory note — otherwise the projection margin between mask and truth would measure a fiction. When only some latitudes failed at the start, the report also notes that per-latitude alpha rows would be the finer declaration (an open item, tracked with the scene-gating work it depends on).

The cap advisor (loop v2, first leg). Beside the exclusion walk, the window now carries the v2 advisor for the per-cell co-frequency cap: it walks the cap down from the profile's effective baseline (the declared cap, clamped by demand), synthesizes per latitude the largest cap that latitude's own outcome allows — with a stay-passing check, since tighter is not always better — then verifies the composed rows in one joint sweep before offering them. Apply writes the rows into the profile's per-latitude cap array, never touching operator rows outside the swept span. When no margin moves over the walk it says so plainly: the cap is not the lever (with one demanded link per cell it barely binds). The exclusion and elevation levers will reuse the same machinery once their payload-side gating clears.

Tech · promotion: from tendency to promise

The alpha walk is one instance of a general recipe, because the filing carries no scheduling strategy — a behaviour reaches the declarations only as an enforced gate. Observe the tendency in the simulated truth; harden it into the profile field the scheduler enforces (MinHoldSec, MinElevDeg, AlphaExclDeg, the Nco caps); verify the truth cost — re-sweep, and run the truth payload under the declared R (the runner's override mode is the before/after instrument); declare it through the R set, now backed by enforcement. Beware the elevation case: derived min_elev rows above the enforced floor are the envelope of one finite run, not a promise, until the floor itself is promoted. And a declared min_duration bites at admission — a link is only made toward a satellite that stays feasible for the whole promised dwell — while flipping the examination to the track-duration algorithm. Today only the alpha promotion is automated.

Tech · the power headroom line

epfd moves exactly dB-for-dB with the per-beam transmit power density — the payload envelope study's linear frontier — so every sweep's worst margin doubles as the TxEirpDbw headroom: how much per-beam power the payload may add (or must shed) at the swept exclusion. The summary prints it after every sweep and at the advisor's endpoints. Live-composition footprint only: a declared mask is a fixed file, and a power change would need the mask regenerated.

Tech · a mask that is given

When the footprint source is a mask — a filed mask examined as it is, or any mask taken as input rather than derived — the loop cannot re-derive it and does not try: gates act on boresights, and the mask’s per-direction maximum has already collapsed which beam produced each cell. It reports instead whether the mask already carries the shaping the R set declares: per latitude block, how far inside the declared exclusion zone and how far below the declared elevation floor power within 3 dB of the block’s peak reaches — CONSISTENT at the gate, MASK TIGHTER beyond it, LIT INSIDE when the reach stops short of the arc, SATURATED when it does not. The examination-only mode runs the same check on every mask it is given. A derived mask read this way shows the main-lobe edge of gated beams reaching inside the zone at its cell size; a filed two-level mask shows its rule.

Where the declaration comes from

The loop no longer ends where the deriver begins — the derivation is the loop’s first step. Before the truth sweep runs, a saturated probe (no victim, no limit, demand at the declared co-frequency cap, activity and duty and operating fraction at 1) measures the R set and the pfd mask. The truth sweep then measures T. A third sweep examines that same truth against the declaration and gives E1. One run, three passes, and the profile, R set and mask are written into one directory so they describe one system.

The R-set designer’s Derive & fill reads that directory rather than simulating again. Where Declarations Come From sets out the whole relation — and why the declaration must not be measured on the run that later verifies it.

What has not changed is the loop’s prescriptive half. Where it walks operating rules — an exclusion angle, per-latitude co-frequency caps — what it finds is written into the profile, as rules the scheduler enforces. Nothing it synthesises is filed directly: a synthesised cap becomes a profile rule, and the filed MAX_CO_FREQ row is then measured from operation under that rule.

The acceptance test

E1 must sit at or above T at every latitude. That is what proves the declaration actually envelopes the system it describes, and the run states it explicitly — naming the latitudes when it fails. An E1 below T is a granularity failure of the derivation, never a compliance failure of the system: one is fixed by measuring more finely, the other by redesigning. The gap between them, where the test passes, is the margin the declaration gives away — and at the same dB-for-dB rate as the power headroom above, it is operating power the system could have been licensed for.

What a run can resolve

The defaults — 0.1 day at 60 s, 144 steps per sweep — are screening-grade, chosen so a fifteen-step walk stays affordable. Short-term limit points at very small percentages need far longer runs to even resolve: a point at 0.017% of the time is decided by a handful of extreme samples, on the order of six thousand steps or more. And the step size matters as much as the count: measured on the reference geometry (450 km cells at 1200 km), 60 s steps miss the main-beam transients entirely — per-run maxima moved ~20 dB between 60 s and 6 s time steps and only ~0.3 dB between 6 s and 1 s — so verdict-grade tail levels need steps of a few seconds, enough to resolve the beam footprint crossing the victim. That is our own measurement of a rule the simulation methodology states generally: tie the time step to the beamwidth of the interfered antenna, so that at least five samples fall while a satellite crosses its 3 dB beam (Rec. ITU-R S.1325 revision draft §2.7.2). It governs a simulation's own stepping; the examination's stepping is S.1503-4's business, not this one's. The loop's job at screening depth is to locate the compliant region, the binding latitude and the trend; the final word belongs to a long, fine-stepped run at the found angle. The window also warns when the profile carries per-latitude exclusion rows the payload's single scene ring cannot express — a verdict computed in that state names its gap rather than hiding it. One more rule governs what a run may quote as an absolute: a figure stands only with a convergence pair — the same statistic at twice the depth, moving no more than a stated tolerance (0.5 dB by default), both depths named where it is quoted. Differences at matched depth are quotable as differences. The pair is bought at half price: the derivation measured exactly depth-stable, so a truth-only rerun (reuse=<run dir>) skips the probe, reads the earlier run’s R set and mask back, and runs only the truth sweep and the examination at the new depth.
