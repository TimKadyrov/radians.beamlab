Inside the Compliance Loop

The Compliance loop window checks whether your system meets the Article 22 limits on downlink equivalent power flux-density (epfd) at a range of earth-station latitudes. It can also derive the declarations your filing carries and check that they cover what the system really does.

Two ways to run

  • Run sweep makes one pass. When the profile simulates its beams live, the pass measures the truth, T: the epfd the system produces as it operates. When the profile’s footprint is a declared pfd mask, the pass examines that mask the way Rec. ITU-R S.1503-4 does, under the R set named in Declared R set or, without one, under the profile’s own gates.
  • Run loop makes three passes. It derives the declaration from the system, or takes the R set you name. It measures the truth T. Then it examines the declaration at the same victims and gives E1. A second table shows T beside E1 for every latitude.

The victims

Every run places a victim earth station at each latitude of a grid, by default 0 to 70° in 10° steps, at the ES longitude you set. Its wanted GSO satellite sits at a fixed longitude offset, 10° east by default, and it has an S.1428 dish. Each latitude becomes one row of the table:

  • max epfd: the highest level seen, or quiet when no satellite contributed at all;
  • point margin and curve margin, in dB, positive when there is room to spare;
  • verdict: PASS or FAIL;
  • deciding point: the limit point that sets the point margin;
  • quiet: the time steps in which no satellite contributed;
  • curve crossing: where the distribution crosses the limit curve, if it does.

While a run goes, the status line shows its progress and the time left. Afterwards it gives the run’s depth: the steps per latitude and the smallest percentage of time the run can resolve. Export table… writes the rows as CSV, headed by the run’s inputs.

Two things the table does not cover:

  • Latitudes between the grid points. The worst margin is the worst over the grid only. On one test system a 5° grid found a latitude 10 dB worse than anything on the 10° grid, so a pass can turn into a fail on a finer grid.
  • Other victim geometries. The run does not search for a worst-case victim: the ES longitude and the GSO offset stay where you set them. On the STEAM-2 test system over two days, moving the GSO satellite was worth about 2 dB at the equator and 1 dB or less elsewhere.

The limit

The sweep judges against the limit points in the text box, one epfd_db percent_time line per point, typed from the applicable Article 22 table. Or load them: point at the BR limits database and press Load, which lists the rows that apply to the profile’s downlink carrier and the shells’ lowest operating height. Pick a row and press Use. It fills the text and sets the victim dish to the row’s reference diameter, the antenna the limit was written for; a run warns when the dish no longer matches. Rows whose limits depend on latitude cannot fit the flat text, so they are shown for you to copy the values for your latitude band. With no limit entered, the verdicts are against a permissive placeholder and the status line says so.

The verdict

A latitude passes only if its epfd distribution stays within the limit at every tabulated point and nowhere crosses the limit curve drawn between the points. Its margin is the smaller of its point margin and its curve margin, and the worst row is the binding latitude.

Tech · the rule and the two margins

The rule is compliance against the Article 22 limit curve, adopted for this dataset. At every tabulated point the level exceeded for the allowed percentage of time must not pass the limit level (S.1503-4 §D7.1.3, over the §D7.1.2 CDF bins). Between the lowest and highest tabulated epfd, the distribution must nowhere cross the limit curve, the log-linear interpolation of percentage between adjacent points, read with a tolerance of half a bin (0.05 dB) towards lower epfd. The gaps between tabulated points are wide, 5.4 to 34 dB in Table 22-1C, and a distribution can clear both ends of a gap yet bulge above the curve inside it; the examination under S.1503-4 fails such a case. The point margin is the limit epfd minus the measured epfd at each point’s percentage, the minimum over the points. The curve margin is the dB shift of the whole distribution that just clears the curve.

Reading the result

A correct declaration covers the system, so E1 is never below T: the examination sees the truth plus the margin the declaration adds. The gap column is that added margin. Three outcomes are possible, and each has its own remedy:

  • T fails. The system itself breaks the limit. Change the system, not the filing: first the per-beam power density, which moves epfd dB for dB and whose required cut the summary prints; then the operating rules the advisors walk; then the constellation.
  • T passes, E1 fails. The system is fine, but its filing would be found non-compliant. Work on the declarations: finer mask grids and a time step fine enough for the limit’s percentages, declarations measured from the simulation rather than generous hand-written values, and behaviour turned into enforced rules so the declarations can be tighter. The aim is declarations that describe the real operation. Passing follows when the system deserves it; the declarations are never fitted to the limit.
  • Both pass. File. The gap is conservatism the filing carries.

E1 below T at any latitude is not good news. It means the declaration does not cover the system it describes, because the derivation was too coarse. Run loop checks E1 ≥ T at every latitude and names the latitudes that fail. The fix is to measure more finely, never to redesign the system.

Where the declaration comes from

Run loop derives the declaration before it measures anything else.

  • The R set is measured on a saturated probe: no victim, no limit, demand raised to the declared co-frequency cap when one is declared, and activity, duty and operational fraction at 1. What the probe observes becomes the declared operating parameters. Name an R set in Declared R set to skip the probe and use that set instead.
  • The pfd mask is not measured. It is the profile’s declared mask when the profile names one; a named file that is missing stops the run, which names it. Without one the run computes the mask from the payload’s reachable envelope: the strongest level any permitted beam position could produce, whether the probe visited it or not, and over the profile’s Yaw steering range when it gives one, so the mask is conservative by construction. Latitude rows from which no cell within the declared service latitudes can be reached are written as no transmission. A later run reuses the computed mask while it is newer than the profile.

The profile, the R set and the computed mask are written into one run directory, so they describe one system. The R-set designer’s Derive & fill reads that directory instead of simulating again. Where Declarations Come From explains the whole relation, and why the declaration must not be measured on the run that later checks it.

Tech · a mask that is given

When the footprint source is a mask taken as input, such as a filed mask, the loop cannot re-derive it: the mask keeps only the strongest level in each direction and has lost which beam produced it. Instead Run loop checks whether the mask already carries the shaping the R set declares. For each latitude block it measures how far power within 3 dB of the block’s peak reaches inside the declared exclusion zone and below the declared elevation floor, and grades each: CONSISTENT at the gate, MASK TIGHTER beyond it, LIT INSIDE when the reach stops short of the arc, SATURATED when it does not. Beside the elevation grade it reports the lit reach, the lowest ground elevation still within 20 dB of the block’s peak, as a number and not a grade: on the one family of masks examined for it, masks built with and without an elevation floor lit the ground down to the same elevations, so it cannot tell a missing floor from a spilling one. A computed mask read this way shows the main-lobe edge of gated beams reaching inside the zone at its cell size; a filed two-level mask shows its rule.

The advisors

Two advisors search for the operating rule that makes the truth pass, judging by the verdict’s own margin. What they find goes into the profile as a rule the scheduler enforces. Nothing they find is filed directly: the filed values are then measured from the operation under that rule.

  • Suggest exclusion raises the global GSO exclusion angle from the profile’s value in steps of alpha step, up to the cap, re-running the full sweep at each step. It stops at the first angle where every latitude passes. It walks rather than bisects because every step is a result: the report gives the worst margin, the latitude holding it, and whether the margin improved, worsened or stayed flat as the angle grew. From that trend it says what to do next: raise the cap, accept that a wider exclusion makes this geometry worse, or accept that the exclusion is not the lever. When only some latitudes failed, it notes that per-latitude exclusion rows would be the finer rule. Apply to profile writes the angle into the profile.
  • Advise Nco rows works on the per-cell co-frequency cap. It walks the cap down from the profile’s baseline, the declared cap clamped by demand, no lower than the Range floor. For each latitude it keeps the largest cap that latitude’s own result allows, checking that a tighter cap does not make a passing latitude fail, and it verifies the combined rows in one joint sweep. Apply Nco rows writes them into the profile’s per-latitude cap rows and leaves rows outside the swept latitudes alone. When no margin moves, it says the cap is not the lever; with one link demanded per cell it barely binds.
Tech · the power headroom line

epfd moves dB for dB with the per-beam transmit power density, so every sweep’s worst margin is also the TxEirpDbw headroom: how much per-beam power the payload may add, or must shed. The summary prints it after every sweep and at the advisors’ end points. It applies to live beams only; a declared mask is a fixed file and would have to be regenerated.

Tech · from tendency to promise

The filing carries no scheduling strategy, so a behaviour reaches the declarations only as a rule the scheduler enforces. The recipe: observe the tendency in the truth; harden it into the profile field the scheduler enforces (AlphaExclDeg, the Nco caps, MinElevDeg, MinHoldSec); verify what it costs by sweeping again, and by running the truth under the declared R set with the simulation runner’s override; declare it through the R set. The two advisors automate the harden step for the exclusion angle and the caps; the other fields are promoted by hand. Two cautions. Derived min_elev rows above the enforced floor describe one finite run, not a promise, until the floor itself is raised. And a declared min_duration makes the scheduler admit a link only toward a satellite that stays feasible for the whole dwell, and asks the examination for the track-duration algorithm of S.1503-4 §D5.1.4.2. That algorithm is not built here: such a set is examined with the classic algorithm, and the run says so.

How long, and how fine

The defaults, 0.1 day at a 1 s step, give 8 640 steps per latitude. That is screening depth: enough to find the compliant region, the binding latitude and the trend. The final word belongs to a long run at the settings found. The exclusion walk costs up to one full sweep per step.

  • Short-term limit points need long runs. A point at 0.017% of the time rests on a handful of extreme samples; resolving it takes on the order of six thousand steps or more. The status line states the smallest percentage each run resolves.
  • The step must be fine. A satellite crosses the victim’s 3 dB beam in seconds, and a coarse step misses the peak. On one test geometry, 450 km cells at 1 200 km, the maxima moved about 20 dB between 60 s and 6 s steps and about 0.3 dB between 6 s and 1 s. On STEAM-2 the truth’s maxima rose 2.0 to 3.3 dB between 60 s and 6 s. So the step is preset to 1 s, and the window warns when a step samples the fastest crossing of the victim’s 3 dB beam fewer than three times.
  • The step can change the traffic. With the Random strategy and no hold, the selection is redrawn at every step, so the step is also the reselection period.
  • The examination has its own step. Set Examination step to S.1503-4 fine/coarse to examine a declared mask on the time step S.1503-4 prescribes, with its dual time step, over the same duration; in Run loop this fills the E1, S.1503-4 step column. On STEAM-2 the examination at 1 s reads within 0.1 dB of that step. The truth and the advisors stay on the preset step.
  • Per-latitude exclusion rows in the profile gate the scheduler only; the beams carry a single exclusion ring. The window warns when a profile has such rows.
Tech · the time-step rule and convergence

Three samples per crossing is our own threshold, where the measured maxima settled. The simulation methodology states the general rule: tie the step to the beamwidth of the interfered antenna, with at least five samples while a satellite crosses its 3 dB beam (Rec. ITU-R S.1325 revision draft §2.7.2). The fastest crossing is the pass time of S.1503-4 §D4.2; the examination’s own stepping is that of §D4. A figure quoted as an absolute needs a convergence pair: the same statistic at twice the depth, moving by no more than a stated tolerance, 0.5 dB by default, with both depths named. Differences at matched depth can be quoted as differences. The pair is cheap: the derivation is depth-stable, so a rerun that names the earlier run’s R set in Declared R set skips the probe, reuses the mask, and runs only the truth and the examination at the new depth.
