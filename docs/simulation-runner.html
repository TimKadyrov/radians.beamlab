Inside the Simulation Runner

The Simulation runner window simulates your system as it operates, for one victim, and writes the statistics of the equivalent power flux-density (epfd) it produces. This page explains what each input supplies and what the window gives back.

What a run computes

epfd is the power flux-density arriving from every transmitter in view, each weighted by the victim antenna’s gain toward it relative to its peak, and summed. A run computes three directions:

  • epfd(down): the satellites’ downlinks, received by a GSO earth station, the victim ES, pointed at its GSO satellite;
  • epfd(is): the same downlinks, received by that GSO satellite;
  • epfd(up): the system’s transmitting earth stations, received by that GSO satellite.

Using the window

  • Pick the Orbit design document and the Operation profile. Both are required. Operating params (optional) takes an R set, a set of declared operating constraints.
  • Set the victim and the run: the GSO longitude, the earth station’s position and dish, the duration and the time step.
  • For verdicts, type the Article 22 limits into Limits (optional).
  • Press Validate inputs. The status line says what a run would cover, or why it cannot run.
  • Press ▶ to watch the operation on the map, or Write CDFs… to compute the statistics.

Orbit design document

A *.orbitdesign.json saved from the Orbit Design tab. It holds the space segment only: each shell’s orbit, planes, phasing and orbit case (see The Three Orbit Cases). The runner rebuilds the constellation from it and propagates every satellite. It carries no payload, power or scheduling, so one constellation can be run under different operations.

The first shell’s operating height, or its altitude when no operating height is set, is the altitude the payload’s beam layout is computed for. The profile has no altitude of its own.

Operation profile

A *.opprofile.json saved from the Operation profile window. It is the operated system in one file:

  • the downlink payload: beam pattern and layout, transmit power, frequency reuse, reference bandwidth;
  • the link rules the system enforces, its gates: minimum elevation, GSO exclusion, co-frequency caps, minimum angles at the satellite and at the earth station, hold before handover;
  • the service geography: latitude and longitude span, cell size;
  • the tracking strategy, demand and activity;
  • the uplink fleet: frequency, power, power control, dish;
  • the Footprint source, which sets how epfd(down) is computed.

The profile is required: the window has no payload of its own to fall back on. A payload field left empty in the profile takes the payload model’s default.

With beam composition (shaped) as the footprint source, epfd(down) comes from the beams the scheduler switches on at each step. With PFD mask (declared XML), it comes from a declared pfd mask instead, read the way S.1503-4 reads it.

Tech · the pfd mask footprint

On this path epfd(down) is computed as in the examination under S.1503-4 (§D5.1.4.1). At each step every satellite visible from the earth station reads its pfd from the mask: the latitude block nearest the satellite’s sub-satellite latitude, read inside by the bilinear interpolation of §D5.1.5. The gates in force, the profile’s or the R set’s, decide which satellites count. The map still plays the scheduler, and epfd(up) still uses it. No .is.csv is written: the in-space direction needs the e.i.r.p. masks, which a pfd mask does not carry.

Operating params (optional)

An R set: a *.opparams.json saved from the Operating parameters (R set) window. When you give one, its declared constraints replace the profile’s gates, for the downlink and the uplink:

  • the minimum elevation;
  • the GSO exclusion angle;
  • the co-frequency caps, per cell and per satellite;
  • the minimum angles at the satellite and at the earth station;
  • the minimum duration of a link, in place of the profile’s hold before handover;
  • the span of earth-station latitudes served.

Everything else still comes from the profile: the payload and its power, the geography, the tracking strategy, demand and activity. Validate inputs then reports gates: declared R set (override).

Use it to see what a declaration costs or allows. Run the same profile twice, once on its own gates and once under the R set derived from it, and compare the two sets of curves. Run loop in the Compliance loop window saves its derived set as a *.operparams.json in its run directory, and the … button lists those files beside the designer’s *.opparams.json.

Tech · how the gates are read

The per-latitude rows are read the way S.1503-4 Part B defines for the R set: the minimum elevation and the co-frequency cap at the nearest latitude row, the exclusion angle by linear interpolation between rows; beyond the last row, the outermost row governs. Each quantity is filed in one form, header value or array. A set that files a quantity in both forms is an invalid filing, and the runner refuses it. A quantity filed in neither form does not constrain.

The victim and the run

  • Victim GSO longitude (degE), default 10: the GSO satellite the victim earth station points at for epfd(down), and the victim satellite for epfd(is) and epfd(up).
  • Victim ES latitude (deg) and Victim ES longitude (deg), default 45 and 0: where epfd(down) is collected. The GSO satellite’s receive beam points here for epfd(is) and epfd(up).
  • ES dish diameter (m), default 0.6: the victim earth station’s S.1428 antenna. It also serves the transmitting earth stations when the profile’s uplink side gives no dish.
  • Duration (days) and Time step (s), preset to half a day at 1 s: 43 200 steps per direction.

The step needs care:

  • It must be fine. A satellite crosses the victim’s 3 dB beam in seconds, and a coarse step misses the peak. The status line warns when the step samples the fastest crossing fewer than three times, and names the S.1503-4 fine step.
  • It can change the traffic. With the tracking strategy random among feasible and no hold, the selection is redrawn at every step, so the step is also the reselection period.
  • Rare events need long runs. A limit point at a small percentage of time rests on the few steps with the highest levels, so a short run resolves it poorly.

Inside the Compliance Loop gives the measured effect of the step and of the run length.

Tech · the antennas and the uplink

For epfd(down) the victim earth station has the Rec. ITU-R S.1428 pattern of the ES dish diameter (m) at the profile’s downlink frequency. For epfd(is) and epfd(up) the victim GSO satellite has a fixed Rec. ITU-R S.672 pattern: 40.7 dBi peak gain, 1.55° half-power beamwidth and a near-in side-lobe level of −20 dB. For epfd(up) each served cell transmits from its centre toward its serving satellite, with the S.1428 pattern at the profile’s uplink frequency and uplink dish.

Power control is on range. The profile’s ES power ceiling (dBW), per reference bandwidth, applies at the slant range seen at Power control ref elev (deg); a shorter link transmits 20 log10(dref/d) dB below it, a longer one at the ceiling. Left empty in the profile, the ceiling is 0 dBW, and the reference elevation is the R set’s header minimum elevation when an R set gives one, else the profile’s Min elevation (deg).

Limits (optional)

Type the applicable Article 22 limit for any direction, one point per line in the form epfd_db percent_time: epfd(down) from Tables 22-1A to 22-1E, epfd(is) from Table 22-3, epfd(up) from Table 22-2.

A direction with a limit gets a verdict under the limit-curve rule. It passes only if its distribution stays within the limit at every tabulated point and nowhere crosses the log-linear curve between them. The verdict, PASS or FAIL with the point margin, the curve margin and the deciding point, goes into the status line and into that direction’s CSV file. A direction without a limit gets no verdict. Inside the Compliance Loop explains the rule and the two margins.

Set ES dish diameter (m) to the dish the limit row is written for; this window does not check it. On the pfd mask path epfd(is) is not computed, so it gets no verdict.

Checking the inputs

Validate inputs reads every input; picking a file with its … button does the same at once. The status line then opens with ready: and gives the shells, the satellites, the victim and the number of steps. It adds, when they apply:

  • that the gates come from the R set;
  • that epfd(down) reads a declared mask, with the mask’s type, latitude blocks and reference bandwidth;
  • a warning when the profile has per-latitude exclusion rows: the scheduler obeys them, but the beams carry only the global exclusion angle;
  • a note when the step samples the fastest crossing of the victim’s 3 dB beam fewer than three times.

When something is wrong, the line opens with invalid: and names it. A run is refused when:

  • the orbit design document or the operation profile is missing;
  • the R set files a quantity in both header and array form;
  • the duration, the time step or the dish diameter is not a positive number;
  • a limit line is not two numbers;
  • the profile’s footprint source is a pfd mask but it names no mask file.

Watching the operation: ▶, ⏩ and ⏹

The map plays one continuous timeline of the downlink schedule. It shows the service cells, the satellites, thin lines for candidate (feasible) links and thick lines for active (granted) links.

  • ▶ advances one step per tick and redraws the map.
  • ⏩ advances 50 steps per tick without redrawing. The last frame stays; only the readout beside the buttons moves.
  • Press the other icon at any moment to change pace. The time and the schedule carry on.
  • ⏹ stops. The next start begins again at time zero.

The readout gives the time in hours and the active, candidate and unserved links. Neither pace collects statistics, and Write CDFs… is unavailable while the map plays.

The statistics: Write CDFs… and View CDFs…

Write CDFs… asks for a base name, then runs the whole duration without drawing: epfd(down) and epfd(is) first, then epfd(up). The status line shows the progress and the time left. The run writes base.down.csv, base.is.csv and base.up.csv; on the pfd mask path there is no .is.csv. Each file is a cumulative distribution (CDF): for every epfd level, the percentage of time it is exceeded.

When the run completes, the status line gives each direction’s maximum epfd and its quiet steps, the steps in which nothing contributed, then the verdicts. The CDF viewer opens over the new curves: epfd on a linear dB axis against the percentage of time exceeded on a log axis, one colour per curve. View CDFs… opens the same viewer over existing CDF files; pick one or more.

The runner judges one victim. To cover a grid of latitudes, and to derive and examine a declaration, use the Compliance loop window. It runs the same downlink calculation at every latitude.

Tech · the CDF files

Levels fall in the 0.1 dB bins of S.1503-4 §D7.1.2. Each file opens with comment lines: the direction; the victim (for epfd(up), the earth stations’ power and reference elevation); the step count, the step and the duration; the reference bandwidth refbw_khz; the quiet steps; the maximum epfd; and, when a limit was entered, the verdict. The data rows are epfd and percentage of time exceeded.

refbw_khz is the bandwidth the file’s levels are in, and the viewer labels its axis with it: the profile’s Ref bandwidth (kHz), except on the pfd mask path, where the downlink file states the mask’s own reference bandwidth, which Validate inputs also reports. The level column is headed epfd_dbw_m2_40khz whatever the bandwidth.
